Settled enough to keep building

  • A card is a booking. The board is a week.
  • Start, complete, defer to next week, and on hold are the moves.
  • Defer moves the booking. On hold is different: when it unblocks, what was actually done, and the remainder is split.
  • A different actual effort updates that booking. Capacity for this week is the point of doing it immediately.
  • Whole-phase complete removes future bookings on that phase. One booking does not ask the question.
  • Overlap between phases is allowed. Overtake is signalled. Nothing is hard-blocked in this cut, so customers can tell us if they wanted the stop.
  • Readiness is not the column name. A job-level yes/no feeds the first phase. Later phases follow the sequence. That rule is the known weak spot.
  • Not every practitioner sees every unassigned booking. For 26.6 that gate is a pool-eligible marker. It is a short-term mechanism, and Boney does not want it to become the real model.

Still open

  • Workflow as the thing that moves Kanban status. The boolean is the fallback. Workflow can sit on top later. Calum still needs a session, with real use cases, before that is designed.
  • What a pool is. US pool work and UK mixed pool-and-advisory are different. Prep can be pooled when sign-off is not. “The pool” is the wrong noun.
  • Automatic allocation, a utilisation buffer under a full week, and advise filling people to the ceiling.
  • A practitioner move that also moves a colleague’s diary.
  • Kanban moves that raise suggestions. For this work the room wants practitioner-led changes. Other service lines may want the opposite.
  • A stored completion date on the phase. Derive it from the last booking.
  • One Gantt row per booking, or a new hours setting.
  • Putting phase dates in Details as the long-term home. Near term they can live in Timeframe. The Gantt is where dates want to be seen.

    Strategic, not a patch

    Integration and automatic allocation

    Completion can be signalled from Dayshape or from the tax workflow tool. Forecast hours still originate in Dayshape for almost every tool except XCM, and that conversation is not ready. Pool allocation that hands someone a week of suitable work, with room left for review notes, is the advise-shaped idea to take to scheduling. A team Kanban for an engagement manager sits with portfolio. Other service lines that want every Kanban move to become a suggestion get an explicit no, or a separate permission, rather than inheriting the high-volume rule.

    Operating picture to keep stable across those releases

    Portfolio is operational responsibility. It can cut across job groups, years, clients, and offices. Job group stays the financial source of truth. They are used together. Steven has already walked Sarah through that. Confirm the table is the one she has before the value team writes the guidance.

    Week blocks are the stance for high-volume compliance. Longer blocks are the stance for advisory and for returns that genuinely run for months. The product survives the other patterns. It should say which one it recommends.

    Practitioner-led for work that just has to flow. Resource managers watch capacity, they do not approve every kick to next week. Suggestions stay for the changes that need that control. GT’s use of suggestions for everything was called out the same morning as a warning, not a pattern to copy.

    Best-practice guidance is a workstream of its own: when to use suggestions, when scheduling is centralised, when a portfolio is the right object. Melissa and Ainsley are the faster route to US resource-management practice than five more customer sessions.
